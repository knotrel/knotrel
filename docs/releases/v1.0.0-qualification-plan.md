## 1. Why and what

The maintainer decided (rc plan §2, §6.1–6.2):
- v1.0.0 gets its **own campaign** against the **latest published 0.9.x,
  frozen as the reference**, **even if the measured code is unchanged**.
- It **reuses the v0.9.0 protocol** (the v0.7.0 qualification-3 method),
  with a perimeter limited to the **relevant comparisons**, without
  reopening every diagnostic.
- Results and intervals are **published on the benchmark site**.
- The v0.9.0 exceptions are **historical evidence for v0.9.0 only**. v1.0
  dispositions are decided on the new results, with **no acceptance in
  advance**. No 0.9 acceptance is transferred automatically.

**This campaign is a reduced selection.** It runs fewer experiments than the
v0.9.0 campaign: no B1–B3, B5, T5 or M_desc unless the core rule triggers
([§4.3](#43-the-core-rule)), and no descriptive second reference. The
method of every cell it reuses is **unchanged**. The one exception is the
A sample size, which becomes fixed ([§5.3](#53-sample-size-of-a-fixed-n--24-deviation-from-v090)).
The selection is fixed by the matrix of
[§3](#3-changed-code-and-cells-exhaustive-matrix) and the perimeter of
[§4](#4-perimeter-a-reduced-selection).

Release code changed since v0.9.0: the D1 fix (feeder delivery sequence 0),
`busy` for exhausted feeder lanes, `too_many_connections` in the error
enum, input byte limits on feeder positions and event IDs, per-lane
admission metrics, `#[non_exhaustive]` on five public types, and the
`router_with_lifecycle` documentation. More may come from R3e;
[§3](#3-changed-code-and-cells-exhaustive-matrix) is recomputed on the
frozen candidate before the protocol is frozen.

| Role | knotrel | Note |

- **A newer 0.9.x.** If a 0.9.x patch is published before the freeze, it
  becomes the reference, as the benchmark site states ("latest released
  0.9.x patch available at campaign freeze", knotrel-benchmarks
  `docs/data/releases.json` `baseline_policy`, `docs/SITE.md`). The freeze
  records its exact tag and 40-character SHA, and it is never changed
  afterwards. The [§3](#3-changed-code-and-cells-exhaustive-matrix) matrix
  is then recomputed against that tag.
- **No descriptive second reference.** v0.9.0 had one (v0.8.0, Part 2).
  v1.0 has none: the reference is also the version users run.
- **Probes.** As in `PROTOCOL §1`, the candidate and reference probe trees
  have **identical source** and differ only in the knotrel version lines of
  the probe lockfiles. Each reference build comes from a clean clone. If the
  v1.0 API (`#[non_exhaustive]`) requires a probe source change to compile,
  it is made before the freeze and is the same for both arms
  ([§13](#13-tooling-work-in-knotrel-benchmarks-not-done)).
- **Release commit rule.** The rc and 1.0 commits must equal the measured
  candidate in `crates/`, `Cargo.toml`, `Cargo.lock` and
  `rust-toolchain.toml`.
  - The only allowed difference is the version fields: the workspace
    version, the `knotrel-core` dependency version and the matching
    `Cargo.lock` lines.
  - Even then, `__TEXT,__text` of the `knotrel-server` release build must be
    identical (`MATRIX invalidation.code_change`). Otherwise the affected
    experiments are repeated.
  - A version string can move the addresses of other strings, and with them
    the instructions that load those strings. So a version-only change may
    still alter `__TEXT,__text`. In that case the rule applies; it is not
    waived.

## 5. Method

The method is the v0.7.0 qualification-3 method as re-run in v0.9.0
(`MATRIX references.method`; `PROTOCOL §2`). It is unchanged except for
the binaries, the campaign directory, the bindings, the perimeter and the
A sample size (§5.3).

### 5.1 Common rules

- **Interval** (`MATRIX rules.interval`): computed on paired log ratios
  `ln(C/B)`. It is the distribution-free interval of the median from
  one-based order statistics: n = 12 → `[r(3), r(10)]`, n = 24 →
  `[r(7), r(18)]`.
  - Coverage is 1 − 2·P(Bin(n, ½) ≤ k − 1) for `[r(k), r(n+1−k)]`. That is
    96.14 % at n = 12 and 97.73 % at n = 24.
  - This assumes independent pairs from a continuous distribution.
- **Classification** (`MATRIX rules.classification`): PASS if the upper
  bound ≤ ln(1+t); REGRESSION if the lower bound > ln(1+t); otherwise
  INCONCLUSIVE. For higher-is-better metrics the ratio is B/C. IMPROVED is
  reported only when the upper bound is below 0, and nothing more is
  claimed from it.
- **Timer limit** (`analyze_lifecycle_qualification.py`): a B cell whose
  median window in either arm is below 1 ms is TIMER_LIMITED. Its numbers
  are kept, and it gets no verdict.
- **No inheritance:** no v0.9.0 or earlier waiver, acceptance or result is
  reused, pooled or used to choose cells (`MATRIX
  rules.no_waiver_inheritance`, extended to v0.9.0).

### 5.3 Sample size of A: fixed n = 24 (deviation from v0.9.0)

**The v0.9.0 rule and its problem.** `MATRIX rules.A` runs 12 pairs per
cell, analyzes them, and adds one 12-pair extension only to cells with an
INCONCLUSIVE gated metric. The extended cell is then classified on all 24
pairs with `[r(7), r(18)]`. That is a two-stage design with an interim
decision. Neither stage's interval was adjusted for it, so the nominal
coverages (96.14 % at the interim, 97.73 % at the end) do not hold for the
procedure as a whole. The Bonferroni bound on the probability that the
reported interval covers is only 1 − 0.03857 − 0.02266 = 93.88 %. Planning
the extension in advance does not change this.

**Option (a), recommended: fixed n = 24 for every A cell, no interim look.**
- Every A cell runs 24 pairs: block 1 (12 pairs per cell, session seeds)
  and block 2 (12 pairs per cell, extension seeds). Both blocks are always
  run for all nine cells.
- **No analysis is computed between the blocks.** The runner's
  `A-analysis` and `A-extension-plan` steps are removed. The analyzer runs
  once, on all 24 pairs.
- **Combination:** the 24 pairs of a cell form one sample of 24 log ratios.
  The block is recorded per pair, but it is not a stratum and is not
  weighted. Pairs are interleaved across cells within each block, by the
  frozen schedule.
- **Interval:** `[r(7), r(18)]` of the 24 ordered log ratios. Coverage is
  1 − 2·P(Bin(24, ½) ≤ 6) = 1 − 2 × 0.01133 = **0.9773**. It is valid by
  construction, because the sample size does not depend on the data.
- **Classification:** unchanged, the rules v0.4.0 classes (PASS,
  REGRESSION, INCONCLUSIVE; INCONCLUSIVE_DISCLOSED or UNRESOLVED). The
  extension rule no longer exists, so "after the extension" reads as "on
  the 24 pairs".
- **Time cost** (from the v0.9.0 attempt-2 file times):
  - A pair takes about 2 s.
  - Each 12-pair block spans three seed sessions, with about 6 min between
    sessions.
  - Main block: 108 pairs, 935 s. Extension block: 24 pairs of two cells,
    765 s, almost all session overhead.
  - Fixed n = 24: two blocks of 108 pairs, about 2 × 940 s plus the chain
    steps, **≈ 33 min** in all. v0.9.0's A took 30.4 min with two extended
    cells, so the cost is about **+3 min**.
- **Why it deviates from v0.9.0.** The deviation is limited to the sample
  size and the removal of the interim decision. The estimator, the interval
  at n = 24, the tolerances, the classes and the seeds are those of `MATRIX
  rules.A` and `experiments.A`. The two v0.9.0 cells that were extended
  (E1, E2) were classified exactly this way at n = 24. The change makes the
  stated coverage true for every A cell, at a cost of a few minutes.

**Option (b), not recommended: a two-stage design with adjusted levels.**
- **Stage 1** (n = 12): classify with `[r(2), r(11)]`, coverage
  1 − 2·P(Bin(12, ½) ≤ 1) = 1 − 2 × 13/4096 = 0.99365 (α₁ = 0.00635).
  PASS or REGRESSION stops the cell. Otherwise it is extended.
- **Stage 2** (n = 24, the 12 pairs of stage 1 plus 12 new ones): classify
  with `[r(6), r(19)]`, coverage 1 − 2·P(Bin(24, ½) ≤ 5) = 0.99339
  (α₂ = 0.00661). INCONCLUSIVE is final.
- **Overall (Bonferroni):** the probability that the interval used for the
  decision fails to cover the median is at most α₁ + α₂ = 0.01296. Coverage
  is therefore ≥ 0.9870, at least the 0.9773 of option (a). This is the
  closest discrete split that does not fall below it: `[r(2), r(11)]` with
  `[r(7), r(18)]` gives only ≥ 0.9710.
- **Cost:** both intervals are wider than in option (a), so more cells end
  INCONCLUSIVE. The runner keeps the interim step. The saving is at most
  one block's session overhead (about 13 min), and only when every cell
  stops at stage 1.

**Recommendation: (a).** It is simpler and has exact coverage. It costs
about +3 min against v0.9.0's measured A, and its budget is deterministic.

### 5.4 Validity, interference and conformance

- **Validity** (`MATRIX rules.validity`): a configuration record is VALID
  only if `errors == 0`, `item_errors` is empty and `noop_mutations == 0`.
  With background activity, `background.failures == 0` and
  `background.completed > 0` are also required.
  - 503 refusals are allowed and reported.
  - Every analyzed M_reg observation must have only status 200.
  - An invalid record is reported, never rerun or dropped. An M_reg cell
    with any invalid analyzed observation is **INVALID** (no verdict).
- **Atomicity** (`MATRIX experiments.B_C.equivalent_references.atomicity_rule`):
  a deciding equivalent cell whose records show `atomicity_equivalent`
  false is reported **NOT_EQUIVALENT**, among the deciding non-PASS
  results.
- **Host** (`MATRIX host_conditions`): AC power and no thermal or
  performance warning, checked before every child; `caffeinate -dimsu`;
  nothing else heavy running (no builds, no other benchmark).
- **Interference policy:** revision 3, proportional (`MATRIX
  conformance.qualification_3.revision_3`).
  - Measured indicators: external CPU, memory pressure, system sleep, AC
    power, thermal warning, telemetry coverage.
  - Limits: `invalid_window_s` 10, `invalid_cpu` 1.5, `ready_window_s` 30,
    `ready_cpu` 0.6, `pressure_max` 1, `sleep_jump_s` 2, `wait_limit_s`
    600, `retries` 2, `min_free_bytes` 3 GiB.
  - User input, busiest processes, swap and load average are recorded as
    context only.
  - Units: A block (at most one restart, `limits.a_block_restarts`), B/C
    cell, R pair, Rmem cell, M_reg pair.
- **Conformance outcome** (`MATRIX conformance.outcome`): any failed check
  stops the chain at the next gate and makes the whole attempt
  **NONCONFORMING**. No experiment of that attempt gets a verdict,
  completed ones included. Data are kept. Every exit path writes
  `logs/CONFORMANCE.txt`, the final Git state and checksums.
- **Q** has no chain gate, as in v0.9.0. It runs only after Part 1 ends
  CONFORMING.

### 5.5 Attempts, invalidation, failures and reruns

- **Attempts** (`MATRIX conformance.qualification_3.attempts_rule`, for a
  new series `v100q`): **one** attempt, **cap 2**.
  - A second attempt is allowed only if the first does not conform (gate,
    budget or recovery exhausted), never because of a result.
  - The first CONFORMING attempt is final, and every directory counts.
  - A second non-conforming attempt ends the campaign for a maintainer
    decision.
- **Code change** (`MATRIX invalidation.code_change`): any change to
  executed knotrel code after the freeze invalidates every experiment that
  ran that code. Documentation-only commits do not. Executed-code identity
  is checked on `__TEXT,__text` of release builds.
- **Tooling change** (`MATRIX invalidation.tooling_change`): any change to a
  probe, collector or analyzer after the freeze invalidates the affected
  experiment.
- **Infrastructure failure, defined.** A failure is an infrastructure
  failure only if all three hold:
  - it is **unrelated to the measured code**;
  - it is **recorded with evidence**: the log line, the system event and
    the time;
  - it is one of the following:
    - a host power or thermal event (`pmset`);
    - disk full or a filesystem error;
    - loss of the local PostgreSQL container or of its loopback network
      (container exit, `docker` error, refused connection to PostgreSQL);
    - a crash of the tooling outside the product (collector, harness,
      load tool, monitor) not caused by a product response;
    - an operating-system kill not caused by the product's memory use.

  It allows **one rerun of that child** (`MATRIX
  invalidation.failed_child`) or, in Q, of that observation. The rerun is
  recorded with its cause. A second failure of the same child is final.
  When the evidence is ambiguous, the failure is **not** infrastructure
  (fail closed).
- **Product failures are results, never rerun automatically.** A crash or
  panic of `knotrel-server`, `knotrel-core` or the probe's in-process
  router, a timeout, a wrong result, a validation failure or an unexpected
  HTTP error is a **result of that cell**, recorded as such:
  - **A, R, Rmem:** the child fails and the chain stops (`MATRIX
    invalidation.failed_child`). The cell is **FAIL**. Cells of that
    experiment that did not run get no verdict and are reported as not run.
  - **B4, C:** as in A. A C cell's C2 check failing is **FAIL** by rule.
  - **M_reg:** the observation is invalid and the cell is **INVALID**
    (`MATRIX rules.validity`).
  - **Q:** the observation counts as measured, with its failure. A server
    crash, a feeder that does not reach `ready` within the protocol's 300 s,
    or a wrong answer gives the criteria using that observation no passing
    value, so they **FAIL**.
  - Conformance failures are neither: they make the attempt NONCONFORMING
    (§5.4) and never use the rerun allowance.
- **Invalid runs** are kept and reported, never deleted or overwritten.

## 8. Consequences for the rc (approved 2026-11-23)

[rc plan §2](v1.0.0-rc-plan.md#2-r1--v10-qualification-campaign-and-dispositions-decided).

1. **Blocking (maintainer's rule, 2026-11-23).**
   - A deciding **REGRESSION or FAIL blocks** `v1.0.0-rc.1`. It is fixed and
     re-measured (rule 5); a disposition does not lift it.
   - An **inconclusive** result (INCONCLUSIVE, INCONCLUSIVE_DISCLOSED,

     accepted for v1.0 with the classification kept and disclosed, or

     deciding cell without a valid verdict cannot be disposed as PASS.

     published, not blocking.
   - **No v0.9 acceptance is inherited automatically**: every v0.9.0
     exception is historical evidence only.
2. **Q3 needs two things.** If Q3 is not PASS, it needs both:
   - an **explicit disposition**;
   - a **documented operational envelope**: the declared supported
     envelope of rc plan §3, `docs/supported-envelope.md`, with the
     measured feeder catch-up numbers of this campaign.

   **The absence of either one blocks the rc.**
3. **E6 remains explicitly unqualified and requires its own, separate
   disposition for 1.0.** It never becomes PASS.
4. **R2 remains diagnostic.** Not running it does **not** automatically
   block the rc. In that case:
   - the envelope is limited to the configuration actually measured (this
     campaign's single host, loopback, the protocol's rates);
   - **no cause is attributed** to the Q3 failure;
   - **no 0.9 acceptance is transferred automatically**.

   R2's own protocol and decision rule are in rc plan §3, maintained
   separately.
5. **A fix changes the candidate.** Every experiment that executed the
   changed code (the `__TEXT,__text` rule) is repeated on the new candidate
   under the same frozen method, as a recorded freeze revision. Earlier
   results stay published as superseded, never deleted. Experiments whose
   executed code is unchanged keep their results.
6. **As measured.** Classifications, intervals and thresholds stay as
   measured. A disposition is recorded next to the verdict, never in place
   of it.
7. **NONCONFORMING twice** (Part 1 or Q): no rc until the maintainer
   decides how to proceed.
8. **Release commit:** the rc and 1.0 commits satisfy the release commit
   rule ([§2](#2-builds)), or the affected experiments are repeated before
   the tag.
9. **Disclosure categories and TIMER_LIMITED cells** do not block. They are
   listed in the release notes.

## 9. Budget and duration

There are **two separate caps, not a shared pool**:
- **Part 1** (the deciding blocks A, B4+C, R, Rmem and M_reg, plus their
  conformance overhead): **≤ 105 min**.
- **Q** (feeder under load, including the Q5 reference arm): **≤ 30 min**.

Time left in one cap is never carried to the other. Block D, if approved,
has its own cap of 10 min, outside both. It runs between Part 1 and Q, and
its outcome affects neither.

**When a cap is exceeded:**
- **Part 1 over 105 min:** BUDGET EXHAUSTED. The chain stops and the attempt
  is **NONCONFORMING** (`MATRIX conformance.outcome`): no Part 1 verdict, and
  data kept. A second attempt is allowed under
  [§5.5](#55-attempts-invalidation-failures-and-reruns), never Q's minutes.
  Q does not run after a non-conforming Part 1.
- **Q over 30 min:** the run stops and keeps the completed observations.
  Block Q is **NONCONFORMING**: criteria whose cells lack their three
  observations get no verdict, and none counts as PASS. Q can be rerun once,
  as a whole, only for this non-conformance or an infrastructure failure,
  never because of a result. A second non-conforming Q goes to the
  maintainer. Part 1's results are unaffected.
- **D over 10 min:** D stops and is reported as incomplete. Nothing else is
  affected.
- Cells are never dropped silently. Raw data, freeze files and the decision
  record are stored in knotrel-benchmarks with checksums, as for v0.9.0.

## 10. Order

1. The R3 changes, including R3e findings, land on `main`, followed by the
   version bump. The [§3](#3-changed-code-and-cells-exhaustive-matrix)
   matrix is recomputed on the candidate. Any new changed item is mapped to
   cells and approved.
2. knotrel-benchmarks: tooling ([§13](#13-tooling-work-in-knotrel-benchmarks-not-done)),
   tests and an independent review, then `FREEZE.json` (revision 1,
   campaign "v1.0.0 qualification"). The core rule is evaluated at the
   freeze.
3. `prepare_campaign.sh`: clean clones, builds, checksums, smoke tests, and
   the launch environment check with dry runs of every collector.
4. One session: Part 1 (A, B4+C, R, Rmem, M_reg), then D if approved, then
   Q. D and Q run only if Part 1 is CONFORMING.
5. Analysis with the frozen analyzers, then `DECISION.md`, the maintainer's
   dispositions, and publication ([§11](#11-publication)).
