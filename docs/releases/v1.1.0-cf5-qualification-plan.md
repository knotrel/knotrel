# v1.1.0 CF5: performance qualification of `cluster-forest` — plan

**Status: Proposed, 2026-11-24. Not approved. No measurement may start
before the maintainer approves this plan (section 12).** Roadmap item CF5;
design in [ADR 0014](../architecture/0014-cluster-forest-backend.md).

## 1. Purpose

CF5 measures the experimental `cluster-forest` engine against `compact-bfs`,
`ett` and `hdt` under the same semantics, workloads, binary and machine, and
classifies every declared cell under rules fixed here, before any data
exists. Its results are the evidence for the CF6 adoption decision; CF5
itself decides nothing about the default engine or the engine's stability.

Questions:

1. On which core workloads is `cluster-forest` faster, equivalent or slower
   than each existing engine, for updates, queries and construction?
2. How do its steady and peak memory compare?
3. Does it change end-to-end server throughput, latency and feeder
   freshness when selected, with and without lifecycle support?

**Not a CF5 input.** The exploratory observation recorded in ADR 0014
section 12 (cut time / log² N on one workload, one machine, no protocol) is
excluded: it is not evidence for the asymptotic bound, it is not reused as a
result, and it did not and must not inform any threshold, cell or size
below. Every number here is chosen from the existing qualification rules
(v0.9.0 and v1.0.0 plans) or from the roadmap's CF5 text.

## 2. Candidate and builds

- **Candidate:** the commit of `main` that contains CF2–CF4, identified by
  SHA in the campaign record before the first observation. The release code
  (`crates/`) must not change during the campaign; a change invalidates the
  campaign (a new candidate and a new run).
- **One binary per harness**, release profile, toolchain 1.100.0, every
  engine in the same binary, so the engines differ only by
  `EngineConfig`.
- `knotrel-benchmarks` pins `knotrel-core`/`knotrel-server` to the
  candidate SHA (git dependency), recorded with `Cargo.lock` hashes.
- **Machine:** one dedicated host, recorded (CPU model, cores, frequency
  policy, memory, OS and kernel), no other workload; the same host for the
  whole campaign.

## 3. Cells

Engines: `compact-bfs` (CB), `ett` (ETT), `hdt` (HDT), `cluster-forest`
(CF). Every comparison is CF against one reference engine; references are
not compared with each other.

### 3.1 Core, plain engine (`ConnectivityGraph`)

| Block | Workloads | Sizes | References | Metrics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| K1 | The eight `knotrel-benchmarks` workloads: `chain-split-rejoin-v1`, `cycle-alternatives-v1`, `components-join-split-v1`, `hub-alternatives-v1`, `sustained-churn-path-v1`, `sustained-churn-blocks-v1`, `dense-bridge-churn-v1`, `redundant-bridge-churn-v1` | 10 000 and 100 000 nodes for the six sparse families; the two dense families at their defined sizes | CB, ETT, HDT | Primary: workload wall time. Report-only: link, cut and query p50/p99 |
| K2 | `staged-insert-delete-v1` (new, section 9): the staged protocol of De Man et al. §8.2 — insert all edges in a seeded random order in 10 stages, then delete them in another seeded random order in 10 stages, with 1 000 queries after each stage (half random pairs, half endpoints of existing edges) — on random simple graphs of average degree 4 and 16 (`E = 2V`, `E = 8V`) | 10^4, 10^5, 10^6 vertices | CB, ETT, HDT | Primary: total update time; secondary: total query time |
| K3 | Construction: `ConnectivityGraph::try_from_edges` of the K2 graphs and of the K1 initial graphs | as K1, K2 | CB, ETT, HDT | Primary: construction time |
| K4 | Memory, deterministic allocator counts (the `lifecycle-probes` counting allocator): after construction, after the last insert stage of K2, after the last delete stage | K2 graphs | CB, ETT, HDT | Live bytes after each phase, peak bytes |

**K2 provenance.** The staged insert/delete protocol is the published
experimental protocol of De Man et al. (§8.2), read for CF1 before any
Knotrel measurement of the engine; the densities (sparse and denser) and the
decimal sizes (aligned with K1) are chosen on those grounds. The exploratory
observation of ADR 0014 §12 also inserted all edges and deleted them; that
similarity comes from the shared published protocol, and its parameters
(`E = 3V`, power-of-two sizes from 2^12 to 2^18) are deliberately **not**
used here.

Sparse, ring and hub graphs are covered by K1 (`chain`, `cycle`, `hub`
families) and K2.

| Block | Cells | References | Metrics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| S2 | S1 with lifecycle on | CB | as S1 |

Core results (3.1, 3.2) and end-to-end results (3.3) are reported in
separate tables and never combined. `cluster-forest` is not presented as a
fix for the Q3 limitation of v0.9.0
([#87](https://github.com/knotrel/knotrel/issues/87)) unless S3 shows it
under the Q rules.

## 4. Method

Timed blocks K1–K3, L1 and S1–S2 use the paired method of the v1.0.0 plan
(section 5.1, option (a) of 5.3):

- **Pairs:** one observation of CF and one of the reference, in a fresh
  process each, order within the pair balanced (12 CF-first, 12
  reference-first) by a frozen seeded schedule; pairs interleaved across
  cells.
- **Fixed n = 24 pairs per cell**, two warmup pairs discarded, no interim
  analysis, no extension. The analyzer runs once, after all pairs.
- **Interval:** distribution-free interval of the median of the 24 paired
  log ratios `ln(CF / reference)` (for throughput `ln(reference / CF)`):
  `[r(7), r(18)]`, coverage 97.73 %.
- **Seeds:** workload seeds fixed per cell and recorded; the order seed is
  drawn once and written into the campaign record before the first
  observation.
- K4 is deterministic: one run per engine and cell.
- L2 and S3 follow their own rules (v1.0.0 plan section 6; feeder protocol):
  three observations per S3 cell, a fresh server and feeder each.

**Correctness gate.** Every core observation checks its answers against the
workload's oracle (the harness's existing independent answers); every
server observation checks `errors == 0` and the validity rules of the
v1.0.0 plan section 5.4. A wrong answer from CF is a **FAIL** of the
campaign: CF5 stops, the defect goes back to CF3, and a new candidate is
needed.

## 5. Classification

Margins (declared now, from the existing tolerances): time `m = 10 %`,
p99 and query tails `m = 25 %`, throughput `m = 10 %`, memory `m = 20 %`.

For each timed cell, reference and metric, with interval `[lo, hi]` of
`ln(CF / reference)`:

| Class | Rule | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FASTER | `hi < ln(1 − m)` | CF is faster by more than the margin |
| SLOWER | `lo > ln(1 + m)` | CF is slower by more than the margin |
| EQUIVALENT | `ln(1 − m) ≤ lo` and `hi ≤ ln(1 + m)` | Within the margin both ways |
| INCONCLUSIVE | any other interval | Not resolved at n = 24 |
| TIMER_LIMITED | median window below 1 ms in either arm | No class; numbers kept |

For K4 (deterministic), with ratio `q = CF / reference`: SMALLER if
`q ≤ 1 − m`, LARGER if `q ≥ 1 + m`, otherwise SIMILAR.

L2 is PASS or FAIL per its rules C1–C3 and EXCEEDS_BUDGET for C4, as in
v1.0.0. S3 reports the Q1–Q5 outcomes for CF and the same-session CB
reference; Q5's reference is CB in the same session.

Every cell is reported with its class, interval and medians, including
SLOWER, LARGER, INCONCLUSIVE and FAIL; nothing is dropped, pooled or
reclassified after collection.

## 6. What CF5 does and does not decide

- CF5 is complete when every declared cell is measured and classified, or
  recorded as not run with its reason (roadmap CF5 completion). It has no
  pass/fail threshold on speed: a SLOWER or LARGER cell is a result, not a
  campaign failure. Only the correctness gate and a NONCONFORMING attempt
  (section 7) invalidate it.
- The results feed CF6. Changing the default engine or promoting
  `cluster-forest` to non-experimental needs evidence on Knotrel's own
  workloads and an explicit maintainer decision (roadmap CF6); CF5 makes no
  such recommendation automatically.
- Existing engines are not re-qualified against v1.0.0 here; the v1.1.0
  release qualification is a separate plan.

## 7. Attempts and validity

- **At most two attempts**: the first, and a second from scratch only if
  the first is NONCONFORMING. Within an attempt, a cell may be rerun
  **once**, only for an infrastructure
  failure (host interference recorded by the conformance checks, a crashed
  collector), never because of its result; the rerun replaces the whole
  cell.
- **Conformance** (as v1.0.0 section 5.4): candidate SHA and binary hashes
  match the record; the host is idle (load average and CPU frequency
  recorded before and after each block); no swap activity. A failed check
  makes the attempt NONCONFORMING: no classes are published from it. If the
  second attempt is NONCONFORMING too, CF5 is reported as not completed, for
  a maintainer decision.
- No rule, margin, cell or size changes after the first observation.

## 8. Budget

Estimated from the v1.0.0 timings (about 2 s per paired core observation at
100 000 nodes). K2 at 10^6 vertices has no prior timing; the dry run of
section 9 measures it, and if the projected total exceeds the cap the plan
returns for approval before the campaign:

| Block | Cells | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K1 | (6 sparse workloads × 2 sizes + 2 dense workloads) × 3 references = 42 cells × 26 pairs | ≈ 1.5 h |
| K2, K3 | 2 densities × 3 sizes × 3 references, plus construction cells | ≈ 2 h (to be confirmed by the dry run) |
| K4 | 6 graphs × 4 engines, one run each | ≈ 0.5 h |
| L1, L2 | 5 cells; 30-cycle churn | ≈ 1 h |
| S1–S3 | 3 + 1 mixes × references; 6 feeder cells × 3 observations | ≈ 2.5 h |

**Cap: 10 hours of host time** for one attempt. Exceeding it makes the
affected block NONCONFORMING (no classes), reported as such.

## 9. Tooling work in knotrel-benchmarks (not done)

1. Add `cluster-forest` to the benchmark engines through
   `ConnectivityGraph` (the backend type is internal), with the versioned
   identity `knotrel-core/cluster-forest-v1`.
2. Add the `staged-insert-delete-v1` workload (K2) with an independent
   oracle.
3. Accept `cluster-forest` in `lifecycle-probes` (`--lifecycle-engines`) and
   in the server load tools (`examples/load`; `examples/measure_e2e.rs`,
   whose engine selector accepts it from this PR, since `crates/` is frozen
   for the campaign).
4. Extend the analyzer with the classes of section 5 (two-sided margins),
   keeping the existing interval code.
5. A campaign script that writes the record (SHA, hashes, host, seeds,
   schedule) before the first observation.

The tooling is reviewed before the campaign; a dry run on a tiny matrix
checks the pipeline end to end and its numbers are discarded.

## 10. Publication

Raw observations, the campaign record, analyzer outputs and checksums are
stored under `knotrel-benchmarks/results/` with the candidate SHA; a
summary table per block (core and end to end separately) goes into the
release notes and `docs/performance-by-version.md`, with no claim beyond
the classes.

## 11. Not claimed

No asymptotic statement follows from CF5; the update bound stays the
project's derivation (ADR 0014 section 6). No comparison with the paper's
C++ implementations or hardware. No blocked or batch-parallel variant
(CF7), and no CF-LCA versus CF-Root comparison.
